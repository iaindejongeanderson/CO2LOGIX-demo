CO2LOGIX UK

Subsurface pressure increases from CO₂ injection can constrain injectivity and safe operating margins, reducing the dynamic capacity of CO₂ geological storage systems. CO2LOGIX evaluates the scale of that pressure buildup under different growth trajectories.

How this model works

Wells are drilled on a logistic schedule: the number started in year t is the derivative of L / (1 + e^(−k(t−t₀))), with t₀ fixed so that two wells exist in year 1. Each well injects at the chosen rate for its injection life, then falls silent.

Pressure

Each well's pressure footprint uses the Nordbotten analytical solution for CO₂ injection into a brine-filled aquifer — a two-region radial solution with a mobile CO₂ plume of radius ψ inside a pressure front at R = √(2.25·D·t). Footprints superpose linearly. Fracture pressure follows Zhang & Yin (2017): p_frac = p_p + 0.75(σ_v − p_p), on 10 MPa/km hydrostatic and 23 MPa/km lithostatic gradients.

Fluid properties

CO₂ density and viscosity, and brine viscosity, come from CoolProp evaluated at each unit's reference pressure and temperature (25 °C/km on a 15 °C surface). Rock compressibility uses the Hall (1953) porosity correlation.

What the numbers mean

The reported peak is the highest reservoir pressure anywhere in the seven storage units, expressed as a percentage of that unit's fracture pressure. It is evaluated at well nodes, where the logarithmic solution peaks — exactly as the published model's grid maximum does. The map shows the same field sampled on a 2.5 km raster, so the raster reads cooler than the well-node peak; well markers carry their own node values.

Well placement

Candidate locations are drawn from a 1 km lattice inside the seven unit outlines, unweighted, so wells land in proportion to storage unit area — the intent stated in example.py: "proportion of total wells to place in each aquifer outline (based on area)".

This is the one place the explorer departs from pressure.py, which weights each lattice point by its unit's area fraction. Since a unit already contributes lattice points in proportion to its area, that weighting applies area twice and places wells in proportion to area squared — starving the smaller units. The Captain Sandstone, the most permeable unit at 7000 mD, would take 0.3% of wells instead of 2.7%, and the thin Dornoch and Heimdal units 2.0% and 0.8% instead of 7.6% and 4.6%.
